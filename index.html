01 — Current / Professional Work
SDU Vikings — Formula Student

Electrical Department: HVD, Strain Gauge Amp, Precharge & Accumulator PCBs

Worked on the university's formula student team's electrical department: designed a new enclosure for the car's High Voltage Disconnect (HVD), designed a strain gauge amplifier for the steering column sensors, revised the Precharge PCB, and I am currently reworking the accumulator's PCBs to improve heat dissipation. All tasks involved CAD - either PCB layout or mechanical design - done in close collaboration with teammates.

SDU Robotics Club — Team Lead

Micromouse Division Lead — Main PCB Design and Battery Charging circuit (TI BQ25886)

I was appointed lead of the university robotics club's newly formed micromouse division, managing a team building a maze-solving competition robot from scratch. Along with organizing and leading the team, I designed the micromouse's PCB - mostly integrating pre-made modules, but along with that I built a custom battery charging circuit around the Texas Instruments BQ25886 IC. At the end I hand-soldered the entire PCB. The mouse was then assembled by the mechanical members of the team and it was then handed over to the programming department for testing and developing the algorithm

Semester Projects — SDU Mechatronics

Rail Cart / Sun-Tracking Solar Panel / Micromouse

Three group semester projects, one per semester, in each I had a different role: The first semester, I programmed a sensor-driven speed-adjusting rail cart; In the second, I programmed and helped CAD-designe mounts for a sun-tracking solar panel; And in the third, I handled the full mechanical assembly for a competition micromouse, and also helped with the design of the PCB.

Tethered Tension Control

Fourth semester project we had to build and control a tethered tension system. As a the person who was in charge of the electronics of the project, I designed the logic board, needed amplifiers, PCBs and power converters. A miniature step-down converter based on the TI TPS54625 was developed from scratch and was put on the drone in order to power it through the tether.

Independent Study — Feb–Jun 2025

Bare-Metal Assembly on a PIC16F18076

I set out to learn assembly language from scratch on a PIC16F18076, working directly from datasheets with almost no tutorials available. Progressed from uploading code, to blinking an LED, to using internal timers and interrupts - each step took roughly a month. This was one of the hardest technical challenges I had tackled independently, and I gained a genuinely deep understanding of microcontroller architecture.

Internship — M2M Services, Bulgaria

PCB Assembly Line & Soldering Lab

Rotated through the full manufacturing process at a security-systems and alarms manufacturer: operating and programming a pick-and-place machine on the assembly line, then hand-soldering THT components in the soldering lab. Also completed an Altium Designer course covering EDA and PCB CAD design during this period.

02 — Personal Projects
Restoration + Custom Build

Retrofitted Soviet Radio → Bluetooth Speaker

Rescued an undocumented flea-market radio by building a custom Bluetooth speaker inside it from scratch - Bluetooth module, amplifier, the radio's original speaker, and a 5V regulator, all on one board. I later upgraded from AA batteries to a rechargeable Li-ion pack with a BMS and a battery level indicator. This is the first electronics project I ever did and it's where my passion for the craft was born.

From-Scratch Build

Arduino RC Remote & Boat

I built a universal Arduino-based RC remote (joystick + radio module shield) and, rather than the obvious car, I built a boat instead - foam hull, 3D-printed motor holders and steering fin, and both transmitter and receiver firmware written from scratch.

Practical / Daily-Use Build

Remote Light Switch

Designed and 3D-printed a housing for a remote-controlled light switch with a manual override button, wired into the junction box above the existing switch. Despite its simplicity, this is one of my most-used and cherished projects to date.

Time-Boxed Challenge — 1 Week

Mini Arcade Guessing Game

Here I self-imposed a one-week deadline to take a project from idea to a finished, enclosed device - including programming, 3D-printed housing design, and final assembly. The final product is a small arcade guessing game.

Discrete-Component Design

Light-Sensitive Siren (No Microcontroller)

Built for a school open-house demo: a light-reactive alarm built entirely from discrete components (LDR, transistor amplifier, relay, 555 astable multivibrator) in a clear acrylic box to show the inner workings - deliberately avoiding a microcontroller to demonstrate fundamentals.

Trade School Thesis Project

Discrete-Logic Temperature Control System

Final project for vocational secondary education (with accompanying 30-page thesis): a device that monitors and regulates the temperature of a container, with overheat protection and heating-element obstruction detection - built without a microcontroller. Because the working schematic is quite simple, I made a flashy housing box with acrylic panels and a fancy control board with an analog tempreture gauge.
